Your aces just got cracked again, and it feels personal. It isn't. All-in preflop against one random hand, AA loses about 1 time in 7. Against three opponents, about 1 time in 3. Knowing the numbers doesn't make losing pleasant, but it helps you judge your play without telling yourself stories.

AA against one or more random hands

Preflop all-in equity for A♠A♥ against 1 to 5 random hands (400,000 deals per row, simulated with the engine behind our poker equity calculator and checked against published tables):

OpponentsAA winsAA losesRoughly
185.0%14.5%1 time in 7
273.1%26.4%1 time in 4
363.5%35.9%1 time in 3
455.7%43.7%almost 1 time in 2
549.0%50.4%more than 1 time in 2

The remainder (about 0.5%) is split pots. So against five opponents, aces lose more often than they win. They're still by far the best hand at the table: each opponent alone only has about a 10% chance of winning the pot.

AA against specific hands

Against KK, the exact figure depends on suits: 81.3% when no suit is shared, 81.9% when both are. Suited connectors crack aces most often: they can make straights and flushes. But at roughly 77% to 23%, getting all your chips in with AA is still a great deal.

How often you're dealt AA

You're dealt AA once every 221 hands (6 combos out of 1,326), about 4 or 5 times per 1,000 hands. That's another kind of variance:

  • over 221 hands, you only have a 63% chance of seeing aces at least once;
  • going 500 hands without aces happens about 1 time in 10;
  • going 1,000 hands without them happens about 1 time in 100.

Losing streaks

Losing two heads-up all-ins in a row with AA: 14.5% × 14.5% ≈ 2.1%. Three in a row: about 0.3%. That sounds rare, but it applies to every possible run of three all-ins. Over a few years of play, with hundreds of AA all-ins, you will go through those streaks. They tell you nothing about your game.

Against KK it's worse: AA loses about 18.6% of the time, so twice in a row about 3.4% of the time.

Variance over thousands of hands

Cracked aces are only the visible part. The real question is: how many hands does it take to know whether you're winning? Estimating that needs an assumption: in 6-max no-limit cash games, a player's standard deviation is commonly around 70 to 100 big blinds per 100 hands. With 90 bb/100 and a player whose true win rate is 5 bb/100 (a solid winner):

Hands playedChance of being down anywayObserved win rate (95% range)
10,00029%−12.6 to +22.6 bb/100
30,00017%−5.2 to +15.2 bb/100
50,00011%−2.9 to +12.9 bb/100
100,0004%−0.6 to +10.6 bb/100

In other words, over 10,000 hands (several months for a recreational player), a good player is down almost 1 time in 3. And a slightly losing player can post a nice result over the same stretch.

Downswings follow the same logic. Simulating 4,000 runs of 100,000 hands under these assumptions, the median worst drop from a peak is about 1,900 big blinds, or 19 buy-ins, for a 5 bb/100 winner. For a 2.5 bb/100 winner it's about 2,400 big blinds. These are ballpark figures: your real standard deviation depends on your style and format.

The math used: over N hands, your result roughly follows a normal distribution with mean win rate × N/100 and standard deviation 90 × √(N/100). Example for 10,000 hands: mean 500 bb, standard deviation 900 bb, probability of being below zero ≈ 29%.

How to live with it

  • Judge the decision, not the result. If you got your chips in with 80% equity, your job is done. The next card says nothing about your decision.
  • Look at your all-in EV graph. Most trackers show what you would have won if every all-in had paid out its expectation. It tells you whether you play all-in hands well, without the noise of the deal.
  • Use a bankroll that can absorb downswings. If a 20 buy-in drop is normal, playing with 10 buy-ins means accepting you can go broke during a perfectly ordinary stretch.
  • Set a stop-loss or a fixed number of sessions. And after a big cooler, take a few minutes' break before your next important decision.
  • Track your mistakes rather than your winnings. Your error rate by spot type settles within a few hundred questions, while your winnings take hundreds of thousands of hands. That's the idea laid out in how to study poker.

Frequently asked questions

How often do pocket aces lose to a random hand?

All-in preflop heads-up, AA wins about 85% of the time and loses about 14.5% of the time, roughly 1 time in 7. The rest is split pots.

What is AA's equity against KK?

About 81 to 82% for AA depending on suits (81.3% when no suit is shared, 81.9% when both are). So aces lose about 1 time in 5.

How often do aces lose against three players?

Against three random hands, AA wins about 63.5% of the time and loses about 36%, roughly 1 time in 3. Against five opponents, AA loses more often than it wins.

How many hands do you need to know your win rate?

A lot. With a standard deviation of 90 bb/100, even 100,000 hands only pin your win rate down to about ±5.6 bb/100. A 5 bb/100 winner is still down about 1 time in 3 over 10,000 hands.
