You have top pair, top kicker, your opponent shoves the turn, and you freeze. Most of the time the answer was already known on the flop. SPR (stack-to-pot ratio) tells you how many bets are left to play, and so whether your hand is already committed.

What is SPR?

SPR is measured on the flop, once preflop betting is over:

SPR = effective stack ÷ pot on the flop

The effective stack is the smaller of the stacks in play: if you have 200 and your opponent has 80, only 80 can go in between you. Example: a 20 pot on the flop with 90 effective → SPR = 90 ÷ 20 = 4.5.

An SPR of 1 means a single pot-sized bet puts everyone all-in. An SPR of 15 means it takes several big bets, over several streets, to get there.

The math that matters: equity to call a shove

Why does SPR decide commitment? Say your opponent shoves their whole effective stack S into a pot P. You call S for a final pot of P + 2S. Just like with pot odds, the minimum equity to call is:

required equity = S ÷ (P + 2S) = SPR ÷ (1 + 2 × SPR)

At a low SPR, the money already in the middle carries a lot of weight: a third of the equity (or less) is enough to call. At a high SPR the threshold creeps toward 50%. More importantly, several bets are still to come: a player who puts 100 big blinds in over three streets has a much stronger range than one who shoves 30 big blinds on the flop.

Example: top pair, top kicker

You hold A♠K♦ on a K♥9♥4♣ flop. Exact equities (every possible turn and river enumerated) against a few hands:

Opponent's handYour equity with A♠K♦
Q♣Q♦ (lower pair)89.6%
K♣Q♠ (same pair, weaker kicker)85.7%
A♥Q♥ (nut flush draw)62.8%

At SPR 1 you need 33%. You're ahead of every one of these hands except the set, and even the biggest draw doesn't push you below the line. You're committed: bet, and call the shove without a second thought. Folding would be a big mistake.

At SPR 12 the question changes. To get 100 big blinds in, your opponent has to bet or raise across several streets. Draws and KQ won't all go that far; sets and two pair will. Top pair is still a good hand, but no longer an automatic stack-off.

A classic spot: the flop pot is 188 and you have 140 behind. SPR = 140 ÷ 188 ≈ 0.74. You need only about 30% equity to call a shove. With top pair, top kicker, losing that hand to a set isn't a punt, it's a cooler. The real decision was made preflop and on the flop.

SPR by preflop action

SPR doesn't come out of nowhere: it follows from what happened preflop. With 100 big blind stacks in cash games (rough figures, blinds included):

Preflop actionFlop potStack behindSPR

3-bet to 9 bb, called19.5 bb91 bb≈ 4.7

Remember the rough shape: single-raised pot = high SPR (above 10), 3-bet pot = medium SPR (4 to 5), 4-bet pot = low SPR (under 2). In tournaments, with 20 or 30 big blinds, even a single-raised pot often has an SPR of 3 to 5.

Practical guidelines

SPR was popularized by the book Professional No-Limit Hold'em (Flynn, Mehta and Miller, 2007). The guidelines that come out of it are starting points, not laws:

  • SPR under 3: top pair good kicker and overpairs are stack-off hands. You want to get the money in, not control the pot.
  • SPR 3 to 6: grey zone. Top pair can go all-in against an aggressive player, but a big raise from a cautious one deserves thought.
  • SPR above 6: to put in the whole stack you usually want two pair or better. With one pair, you're playing to win a medium-sized pot.

These lines move with the opponent: against someone who bluffs a lot you can commit lighter; against someone who only raises the top of their range, less.

How to use it at the table

  • Work it out when the flop lands. Effective stack ÷ pot, a rough division is enough: "I have about one pot left" or "I have five pots left".
  • Make your plan right away. If SPR is low and your hand is strong enough, plan to get it in: betting and then folding to a raise with half a pot left is almost always a mistake.
  • Set it up preflop. Your 3-bet size sets the flop SPR. With a hand that likes low SPR (a big pair), a larger 3-bet makes the postflop decisions simpler.
  • Mind the effective stack. Against a player with 30 big blinds, your 200 stack doesn't matter.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate SPR in poker?

Divide the effective stack (the smaller stack in play) by the size of the pot on the flop. Example: 90 effective with a 20 pot on the flop → SPR of 4.5.

At what SPR is top pair committed?

Below about 3, top pair good kicker is usually a stack-off hand: you only need 33 to 43% equity to call a shove, and it has that against most ranges. Above 6, you generally want two pair or better to get the whole stack in.

How much equity do you need to call a shove at a given SPR?

Required equity = SPR ÷ (1 + 2 × SPR). That's 25% at SPR 0.5, 33% at SPR 1, 40% at SPR 2, 43% at SPR 3, and it approaches 50% as SPR grows.

What is the typical SPR in a 3-bet pot?

With 100 big blind stacks and a called 3-bet to about 9 big blinds, the flop pot is around 19 big blinds with 91 behind: an SPR of about 4 to 5. A single-raised pot is above 15; a 4-bet pot drops below 2.
