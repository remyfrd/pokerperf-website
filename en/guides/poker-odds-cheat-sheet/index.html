Outs → chance to hit

Three different questions, three columns:

  • Turn: one card, from the flop (outs ÷ 47).
  • River: one card, from the turn (outs ÷ 46).
  • Flop → river: two cards, 1 − C(47 − outs, 2) ÷ C(47, 2).
The trap to avoid. The "flop → river" column (and the ×4 shortcut) only applies when you're sure to see both cards, meaning your opponent is all-in. Facing a bet with more betting to come, your call only buys one card: compare the price with the "turn" or "river" column.

Bet size → pot odds → required equity

Required equity = amount to call ÷ (pot + opponent's bet + your call). The same values as in our pot odds guide:

Example: on the turn, your opponent bets half pot (25% required) and you have a flush draw (19.6% on the river). On direct odds it's a fold — unless you expect to win more when you hit: that's what implied odds are for. For a specific spot, use the pot odds calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What are the odds of hitting a flush draw?

With a flush draw (9 outs): 19.1% on the turn from the flop, 19.6% on the river from the turn, and 35.0% from flop to river if you see both cards.

What equity do I need to call a half-pot bet?

25%: you pay 1 to win 3 (3-to-1 pot odds). For a pot-sized bet, 33.3%; for a third of the pot, 20%.

When should I use the flop-to-river column?

Only when you're sure to see both the turn and the river, typically when your opponent is all-in. Otherwise your call only pays for one card, and you should use the turn or river column.
