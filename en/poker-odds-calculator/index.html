What is equity in poker?

Your equity is the percentage of the pot you'd win on average if the hand went to showdown, given the known cards. 50% is a coin flip; 80% means you're a big favorite. It's the basis of almost every decision: whether to call, bet or fold depends on your equity versus what the hand costs you.

How to calculate equity in poker

Equity is a simple count: out of all the ways the remaining cards can fall, how often does your hand win? On the flop, the calculator deals out every possible turn and river (about a thousand combinations against a known hand) and counts your wins and ties — that's an exact result. Preflop there are far too many boards, so it simulates tens of thousands of random ones instead (±0.4%).

At the table, you estimate it in your head: count your outs (cards that improve you) and apply the rule of 4 and 2 below. A few equities worth knowing by heart:

Matchup (preflop)Equity
Pair vs two lower cards (e.g. 99 vs 87s)≈ 81% / 19%
Pair vs two overcards (e.g. 22 vs AKo)≈ 53% / 47%
Higher pair vs lower pair (e.g. KK vs QQ)≈ 81% / 19%
Dominated hand (e.g. AK vs AQ)≈ 74% / 26%

Equity vs pot odds: should I call?

Your pot odds tell you the minimum equity that justifies a call (our pot odds calculator works it out for any bet size). The rule: you call when your equity beats your pot odds.

Example. There's $100 in the pot and your opponent bets $50. You can win $150 by paying $50, so your pot odds = 50 / (150 + 50) = 25%. If he's all-in, your flush draw sees the turn and the river (≈ 35% equity): you're above the threshold, calling is profitable.

The classic trap. If your opponent is not all-in, your call only buys the turn: one card, ≈ 19% (rule of 2). 19% < 25% → on direct odds, you fold. Only implied odds (what you'll win on later streets when you hit) can justify that call.

Counting outs: the rule of 4 and 2

An out is a card that improves your hand. To estimate your chance of hitting without a calculator: multiply your outs by 4 after the flop (two cards to come), or by 2 for a single card. It's an approximation; the calculator above gives the exact figure.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my equity in poker?

Enter the hands and the board in the calculator above: it enumerates the cards to come and returns your exact equity from the flop onward. In your head, use the rule of 4 and 2 on your outs for a good approximation.

What's the difference between equity and pot odds?

Equity is your chance of winning the hand. Pot odds are the equity threshold a call must clear to be profitable. You call when your equity beats your pot odds.

Is this equity calculator accurate?

Yes: from the flop it enumerates every card to come (exact calculation). Pre-flop, where combinations are too many, it uses a very precise Monte-Carlo simulation (±0.4%), shown below the result.

How many outs for a flush draw?

Nine outs (13 cards of the suit minus the 4 already visible), about a 35% chance to complete by the river from the flop.
