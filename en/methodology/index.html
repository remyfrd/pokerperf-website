Methodology: how our numbers are checked

A poker calculator is only as good as its numbers. Here is, in plain words, how our tools compute, how we check they're right, and what they can't do.

1. How equity is calculated

The equity calculator runs on an engine written for PokerPerf. For every way the remaining cards can fall, it builds each player's best 5-card hand out of 7, finds the winner (ties included) and counts.

  • From the flop: exact. The engine goes through every card to come — 990 turn + river combinations heads-up on the flop, 44 on the turn. The result has no margin of error.
  • Pre-flop: simulation (Monte-Carlo). There are more than 1.7 million possible boards, so the calculator deals 30,000 at random. The margin is about ±0.6 point (95% confidence interval): "53.1%" reads "between 52.5 and 53.7%".
  • "Random hand": always simulated, since the opponent's hand changes with every deal.

Under every result, the calculator shows which mode was used (exact or simulation) and how many cases were examined.

2. Three independent checks

An engine that checks itself proves nothing. So we test it against three references that don't share its code:

CheckWhat is verified
The app's engineA set of reference spots, computed by the Python engine used on the PokerPerf app side, is recomputed; both engines must agree.
Published equitiesBenchmark matchups every player knows (PokerStove, Equilab): AA vs KK ≈ 82%, AK suited vs QQ ≈ 46%, 22 vs AK ≈ 53%, AA vs a random hand ≈ 85%, 72 offsuit vs a random hand ≈ 35%…
A second evaluatorA hand evaluator written separately, with a different method, must return the same category on 20,000 random 7-card hands and the same winner on 20,000 showdowns (kicker tie-breaks included).

These checks are part of a suite of 21 automated tests run on every change to the engine, before anything goes live.

3. Hand probabilities

The probabilities on our hand rankings page (chance of ending with a flush, a full house…) are Texas Hold'em probabilities: your best 7-card hand by the river. We recomputed them with our engine over 4 million simulated hands, and they match the published reference values (e.g. one pair 43.8%, flush 3.0%, four of a kind 0.17%).

4. The other tools

  • Pot odds: an exact formula — bet to call ÷ (pot + bet + your call). No approximation.
  • Rule of 4 and 2: a mental-math approximation, presented as such, with the gap to the exact value. The ×4 assumes you will see both cards (opponent all-in).
  • Semi-bluff EV: a deliberately simplified model — one betting round, no raise, equity realised when called. It gives a ballpark to decide, not a to-the-cent value.

5. What our tools don't do

  • They don't decide for you. Equity tells you your chances, not what your opponent will do; pot odds give the threshold, not the implied odds.
  • No range input yet: the calculator compares specific hands (or a random hand), not a range of hands.
  • No promise of winnings. Understanding the numbers helps you decide better; it guarantees no result.

Found a mistake?

If a number looks wrong, tell us via the Support page: we check it, fix it, and the page's update date changes accordingly.
