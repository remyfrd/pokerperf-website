What is a semi-bluff?

A semi-bluff is a bet with a hand that isn't made yet but can improve — typically a flush or straight draw. You have two ways to win: your opponent folds right away, or he calls and you hit your draw.

How the EV is calculated

We add up both scenarios, weighted by their probability:

  • He folds (fold equity): you win the pot.
  • He calls: the pot grows by both bets and you win it with your equity. Your average result = equity × (pot + 2 × bet) − bet.

Example. Pot 100, you bet 50 with a flush draw (≈ 35% equity). He folds 30% of the time. When called, your EV = 0.35 × 200 − 50 = +20. Total EV = 0.3 × 100 + 0.7 × 20 = +44, better than checking (≈ +35).

Break-even fold equity

It's how often your opponent must fold for the semi-bluff not to lose money. If your draw is already profitable when called, it's 0%: you can bet even against someone who never folds. The less equity you have, the more fold equity you need — which is why you semi-bluff mostly with big draws.

Frequently asked questions

When should you semi-bluff?

When your draw has enough equity and your opponent can fold. The more outs you have, the less fold equity you need.

What is fold equity?

The part of your EV that comes from your opponent folding. It depends on how often he folds and on the pot size.

Is the calculation exact?

It's a simplified model (one betting round, no raise). It gives a reliable ballpark to decide, not a to-the-cent truth.
