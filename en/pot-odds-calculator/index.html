What are pot odds?

Pot odds compare what you can win to what the call costs. They translate into a required equity: the minimum share of the time you must win the hand for a call to be profitable. The rule: you call when your equity beats the required equity.

How to calculate them

Required equity = bet to call ÷ (pot + bet + bet). Example: a $100 pot, a $50 bet → 50 / (100 + 50 + 50) = 25%. You just need your actual equity to beat that threshold.

From outs to decision. Not sure of your equity? Count your outs and use the rule of 4 and 2 (×4 on the flop, ×2 for a single card). A flush draw = 9 outs ≈ 36% on the flop: above 25%, you call. The calculator does it for you via the "my outs" option.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate pot odds?

Divide the bet to call by the final pot (pot + the bet + your call): the result is the minimum equity required for a call to be profitable.

What equity do I need to call a flush draw?

A flush draw is worth about 36% on the flop (9 outs, rule of 4). You call whenever the required equity is below that.

Pot odds vs equity — what's the difference?

Equity is your chance of winning the hand; pot odds set the threshold to beat. You call when your equity beats your pot odds.
