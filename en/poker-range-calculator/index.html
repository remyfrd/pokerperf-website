What does an X% range mean?

Preflop there are 1,326 two-card combinations. A 10% range is therefore about 133 combos, not "17 hands out of 169": hands don't all carry the same weight.

Hand typeCombos per handNumber of handsTotal
Pair (e.g. JJ)61378
Suited (e.g. AKs)478312
Offsuit (e.g. AKo)1278936

Direct consequence: AKo alone (12 combos) weighs as much as two pocket pairs. That's why you think in combos as soon as you talk percentages.

Worked example: what is a 4% 3-bet range?

You often hear "a 4% 3-bet is AA, KK, QQ, JJ, AK". Let's count: 4 pairs × 6 = 24 combos, plus AK = 4 suited + 12 offsuit = 16. Total: 40 combos, or 3.0%. A true 4% is 53 combos, so there's room for 13 more combos.

What the tool shows at 4%. Ranked by equity, the top 4% is 88+, AQs+: AA down to 88, plus AKs and AQs — 50 combos (3.8%). It adds TT, 99, 88 and AQs… but not AKo, which only comes in around 5.4%. A real 3-bet range of similar size looks more like TT+, AQs+, AKo: also 50 combos, chosen differently. Try both: click "JJ+, AK", then add TT and AQs by hand.

Takeaway: a percentage tells you how many hands, not which ones. Two players who both 3-bet 4% can have very different ranges.

Blockers: your cards remove combos

The cards in your hand can't be in your opponent's. Pick them under "Remove your hand's blockers" and the grid recounts every hand. Example holding A♠ K♦:

  • AA: only 3 aces left → 3 combos instead of 6.
  • KK: only 3 kings left → 3 combos instead of 6.
  • AK: 3 aces × 3 kings = 9 combos instead of 16 (2 suited, 7 offsuit).
  • QQ, JJ: unchanged, 6 combos each.

Result: the AA-JJ + AK range drops from 40 to 27 combos, out of 1,225 hands still possible. Your opponent's premium hands become much rarer — which is exactly why holding an ace matters when you raise. For more on these numbers, see poker math.

How the tool ranks hands (and its limits)

"Top X%" follows the same convention as PokerStove or Equilab: the 169 hands are ordered by their preflop all-in equity against one random hand. AA comes first (≈ 85%), 32o comes last (≈ 32%). The values come from our own engine (200,000 samples per hand, about ±0.25 points; see our methodology). Other tools may swap a few very close hands.

Threshold rule: hands are added in order while the total stays at or below X%. The hand that would cross the line isn't included — hence 3.8% shown for a 4% slider.

Keep in mind: equity vs a random hand ≠ playability. This ranking favours pairs and offsuit aces and underrates suited connectors (87s, 98s), which win little all-in but make straights and flushes. The "UTG / CO / BTN" presets use the ballpark widths from our preflop ranges guide, but real opening ranges contain more connectors. For each hand's real value, see the starting hands chart.

Frequently asked questions

How many poker hand combos are there?

1,326 two-card combinations, grouped into 169 hands: a pair has 6 combos, a suited hand 4, an offsuit hand 12.

Which hands are in a 4% range?

4% ≈ 53 combos. Ranked by equity, the tool gives 88+, AQs+ (50 combos). AA-JJ + AK is only 40 combos, or 3%.

Why does 88 rank above AKo?

Because the ranking uses all-in equity against a random hand, where a medium pair wins slightly more often than AKo. In real play AK often plays better: it's a limit of the ranking, not a rule of strategy.

How do my cards change my opponent's combos?

Each card you hold removes every combo that uses it. Holding A♠ K♦, AA goes from 6 to 3 combos, KK from 6 to 3, and AK from 16 to 9.

Are the UTG, CO and BTN presets GTO ranges?

No. They are approximate widths (≈ 15, 27 and 45%) filled by equity. A real opening range picks its hands differently, especially suited connectors.
