Poker hand strength: how strong is your hand?

The ranking tells you which category beats which — but hand strength at the table is relative. A pair of aces is the 9th of 10 categories, yet preflop it's the best possible starting hand. A flush is excellent, unless the board shows four cards of the same suit. Always ask two questions: what do I have, and what can my opponent have that beats it?

  • Pair or worse: ~61% of Hold'em hands by the river — the everyday showdown.
  • Two pair to straight: ~33% — solid made hands, but vulnerable on draw-heavy boards.
  • Flush and above: ~6% — you're usually way ahead.

Tie-breakers: same hand, who wins?

  • Two pair vs two pair: compare the higher pair, then the lower pair, then the 5th card (kicker). K-K-4-4 beats Q-Q-J-J.
  • Straight vs straight: the higher top card wins. A-2-3-4-5 (the "wheel") is the lowest straight.
  • Flush vs flush: compare the highest card, then the next, and so on. Suits never break ties.
  • Full house vs full house: the three-of-a-kind decides first.
  • Exactly the same 5 cards: the pot is split.

How to read Texas Hold'em hand rankings

The order comes from how rare each 5-card hand is in a deck: the rarer the combination, the stronger it is. When two players share the same category, the higher cards (then the kickers) break the tie.

The probabilities shown are for Texas Hold'em: the chance that your best 7-card hand (your 2 cards + the 5 on the board) lands in that category by the river. With 7 cards some frequencies flip — you make a pair (43.8%) more often than nothing at all (17.4%), and three of a kind slightly more often than a straight — but the ranking itself never changes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best possible poker hand?

The royal flush (A-K-Q-J-10 of the same suit). Nothing beats it.

Does a flush beat a straight?

Yes. A flush (five cards of the same suit) is rarer, so it beats a straight (five cards in a row).

Three of a kind or two pair — which wins?

Three of a kind beats two pair.

Who wins with the same hand?

Compare the cards of the combination, then the kickers; if everything is identical, the pot is split.
